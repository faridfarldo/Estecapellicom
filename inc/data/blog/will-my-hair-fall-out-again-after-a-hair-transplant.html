Initial Hair Shedding After Transplant

After surgery, the first hairs that grow from the transplanted follicles will indeed fall out, and this is completely expected — it is a natural part of the hair transplant process.

What Is Shock Loss?

Shock loss refers to the sudden shedding phase occurring around two weeks post-surgery. All implanted hairs shed rapidly during this period, but this is normal and expected. Patients are informed beforehand about this recovery stage. Following shock loss, a new growth cycle begins with permanent hairs emerging.

Watching the gradual changes can be exciting, as transplanted hairs initially appear thinner but strengthen over time. Complete results typically develop within 6 months to 1 year.

When Will My New Hair Grow After Shock Loss?

New permanent hair generally emerges within approximately two months on average, though timing varies between individuals. In a successful hair transplant procedure, we expect every transplanted graft to survive. Some follicles may temporarily enter dormancy after shock loss, which is completely normal and part of the natural regeneration cycle.

Will My New Hair Fall Out Again After a Hair Transplant?

The answer is definitive: post-shock-loss hairs are permanent and won't fall out, because the follicles taken from the donor area are genetically resistant to hair loss.

The back of the head contains follicles lasting a lifetime. Since transplanted grafts originate from this donor region, the new hairs that grow after a hair transplant are also highly resistant to future hair loss.
